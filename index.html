Before you start

Four things worth holding in mind, whatever the discussion is about.

Discussions about attendance, performance, and behavior are how team members learn where they stand. Continuous feedback enables team members to assess their development and gain insight into areas of improvement and advancement.

Assume they can improve

A performance discussion is not meant to leave someone feeling scared, inadequate, or devalued. Go in assuming improvement is possible — the goal is to help them get there.

Let them speak first

Give the team member room to reflect on their own performance before you deliver yours. How they describe the situation tells you a great deal about their self-awareness and what they understand the job to require.

Bring specifics, not impressions

Dates, times, what was observed, who else was involved or affected. Specifics keep the conversation on deliverables and results. Generalizations invite argument about whether the thing even happened.

Say what support looks like

Name the coaching, tools, or resources you’ll provide. A discussion that only describes a gap is a verdict; one that describes a path is an opportunity.

Where things live

The left sidebar has two views: By Team Member, where discussions are created, and By Department, which shows a whole department at once with the same actions available. By Team Member opens by default.

By Team Member vs. By Department

Both views offer the same actions on a discussion. They differ in how you find one.

  1. By Team Member

    Search for a person and see everything about them: their info panel, discussion counts, and full history across four tabs.

    This is the only place a discussion can be created.

  2. By Department

    Pick a department and a timeframe to see everyone’s discussions at once, in the same four tabs. Each row names the team member it belongs to.

    Edit, cancel, send a document, and approve all work here exactly as they do in By Team Member.

By Department only offers departments you have access to, and within one you only see team members within your scope.

Step-by-step process

Five steps, start to finish. Open any one of them — who holds the ball at each stage is on the row.

Step 1

Something happened

Who takes action: ManagerWhere this happens: Nothing in the app yet

You saw, heard about, or were told about something a member of your team did.
Potential reasons for a performance discussion record

Before you continue, assess if there is anything to act on, and if a performance discussion is the right course of action.

  • Attendance or job abandonment. A courier clocked in more than thirty minutes late on three shifts inside two weeks. A pharmacy tech didn’t show and didn’t call on three occasions.
  • Performance. Three delivery exceptions on one route in a week, all avoidable. Incomplete delivery notes. Task volume or quality slipping on the same issue you already coached on.
  • Behavior. Unprofessional interactions with teammates. A curt tone with a patient on a recorded call.
  • Misconduct or policy violation. Patient information discussed where it could be overheard. A delivery marked complete that wasn’t. Failure to return an undelivered order to the pharmacy.
  • Positive customer feedback. A customer called in after a delivery to say the courier had communicated with them kindly and proactively in preparation for the delivery. They named the courier and asked that someone be told.

STOP!

Before you continue, check for any of these flags.

If any of them apply, contact peopleops@capsule.com before you take any other step. Don’t create a record, and don’t have the conversation yet.

  • It needs investigating first. Harassment, discrimination, threats, safety, or anything that needs looking into before anyone is told anything. Those are investigations, not discussions, and they follow a separate process.
  • They recently raised concerns about leave or accommodations. The person reported a concern, requested leave or an accommodation, or discussed changes to working conditions. That doesn’t mean you can’t address performance. It means a second set of eyes goes on it first.
  • You want a second opinion. Anything that makes you hesitate. That instinct is usually right, and you don’t need a draft or a written summary to make the call. Call first, write second.

Reminder: termination notices
Say nothing to the team member yet

A termination notice goes to the People Team before the final conversation, not after. Having the final conversation before the record is approved can carry unnecessary risk.

The attestation is different too. A termination notice adds a confirmation that the team member is not engaged in protected activity. Read it rather than clicking past it.

There’s no action plan section on a termination notice, and the document doesn’t send on approval — you have the conversation first, then send it yourself. Step 4 covers that.

Protected activity

Reporting harassment, discrimination, or a safety concern; filing or supporting a complaint; requesting leave or an accommodation; discussing pay or working conditions with coworkers; taking part in an investigation.

If any of that might apply, or you’re not sure, stop and contact peopleops@capsule.com before you check it.

Step 3

Review

Who takes action: People Team (Pending Approval), Manager (Sent Back)Where this happens: Performance Discussion Actions

You submitted, and if required, the record gets sent to the People Team for review.
People Team only

If it’s Pending Approval

Records waiting on you sit on the In Progress tab, with a status of Pending Approval. You can reach them in Performance Discussion Actions via:

  1. By Team Member

    Search for the person, open Review Existing Discussions, and their record is on the In Progress tab. Use this when you know whose record you’re looking for.

  2. By Department

    Load a department and you’ll see a summary of every discussion by category, including In Progress.

    Use this when several are waiting. You can work through one department’s queue in a single pass rather than searching name by name, which also makes it easier to see whether similar situations are being handled consistently.

Two ways to act on it

  1. Edit/Review — stay on the page

    Opens the record’s details in place, below the list. Read it through, then click Approve without leaving the page.

    This is the faster route when the record is clearly complete and you have nothing to ask the manager.

  2. View — open the record in a new tab

    Approve from the Actions button at the top right.

    Opening the record also gives you the comments section, where you can talk to the manager directly by tagging them. If something is missing, Send Back is on the same Actions button.

Hint: use View whenever the record isn’t ready

Comments and Send Back both live on the record itself, so anything that needs a conversation with the manager has to go through that tab rather than the in-page view.

If it’s Sent Back

If your submission is sent back, you will receive a notification. It means something specific is missing, and the record can’t go out to the team member until it’s complete. Everything you submitted so far has been saved.

  1. Read the comments

    Open the record to view the comments, which is where the People Team will explain what else is needed.

  2. Fix the section they named

    Find the record under Review Existing Discussions and click Edit/Review.

  3. Save that section again

    Validate & Save, then re-check the attestation and Submit.

If the comments call out the warning level rather than the content, the record has to be canceled and recreated — level locks at creation. You can cancel your record and reference the canceled details under Review Existing Discussions > Canceled.

If you need clarification, use the comments to reply to the People Partner.

Step 4

Finalizing the record

Who takes action: System or ManagerWhere this happens: Performance Discussion Actions, then their Rippling profile

The record was approved, if needed, and the record moves to finalized.
For everything except a termination notice

The approval notification comes via email, and the document sends to the team member automatically.

You’ll find the record on the Active tab under Review Existing Discussions, with the finalized date and the monitoring end date on the row.

For a termination notice

The approval notification comes by email, with a link straight to the record and the instructions for sending it. Then follow the steps below.

  1. Have the conversation

    Directly, and in person if possible.

  2. Send the document

    Open the record from the link in the email, or find it on the Active tab. Click Send Document, then confirm.

  3. Schedule the offboarding

    Go to the team member’s profile, open the menu at the top right — the three dots — and click Schedule Offboarding.

    You don’t need to attach offboarding documentation if it has already been sent through Rippling. The termination notice you just sent counts.

    Once you submit, the offboarding request goes to the People Team for review and approval. Final pay, equipment, access and benefits are handled from there.

Hint: confirm the document only once.

Clicking it a second time generates it again. Sending the document before the conversation notifies them before you’ve had the chance to. There’s no undo.

Next step: monitoring period — Manager

What to do during the monitoring period, if there is one.

Step 5

What comes next

Who takes action: ManagerWhere this happens: Your 1:1s and the Data Dashboard

A monitoring period you track, an offboarding, or nothing further.
Positive feedback: nothing further

There’s no monitoring period and nothing to track. It shows in the team member’s history alongside everything else, which is the point — when someone later reads this person’s record, the good is there with the rest of it.

Termination: the offboarding request

The discussion record is complete and the offboarding request is with the People Team. What’s still yours is anything the request asked you to confirm, telling your team in whatever way you’ve agreed with the People Team, and covering the routes, shifts, or open work that were theirs.

If anything about the conversation was unexpected — a claim, a complaint, a raised concern — tell the People Team the same day, even if you think it’s minor.

Memo or warning: the monitoring period

The period runs from the date you had the conversation, and the end date is on the record. During this period, the record is yours to track.

  • Track the specific things the record named.
  • Raise it at your regular 1:1s rather than saving it up. The person should never be guessing whether they’re on track.
  • Deliver the support you committed to in the action plan. If you named coaching or a check-in, do it.
  • Record anything new at the time it happens. A monitoring period doesn’t pause the process.

If it gets worse during the period, that’s a new record at the next level, not a note on this one. Go back to step 1.

When the period ends

The record moves from Active to Archived on its own and nothing announces it. Nobody is notified, including the team member — which is why the last step is yours.

Tell them where they landed. Someone who has spent 90 days working their way out of a warning should not have to guess whether it worked.

Create a performance discussion record

Go to: Performance Discussion Actions > By Team Member

Then follow the steps below.

  1. Select the team member

    Use the search field at the top of Performance Discussion Actions.

  2. Confirm their information

    Title, department, manager, work location, start date, attendance policy, and discussion counts appear in the panel. If anything’s wrong, fix it on their profile before you continue.

    The panel takes a moment to populate, and the action buttons stay disabled until it’s ready — that’s there to make sure you’re acting on the correct team member.

  3. Pick type, reason, and level

    If you’re unsure which combination fits, use Which record do I create? Reset Type, Reason, and/or Level while you’re still deciding.

    Once you click Begin New Discussion these are written to the record and locked.

  4. Fill in each section

    Only the sections that apply to your type and reason appear. Every text section needs at least 100 characters.

    Each card has its own Validate & Save. When it saves, the button becomes Edit and the fields lock — that’s your confirmation it went through.

    Editing a saved card unlocks it again, so you’ll need to save it a second time.

  5. Record the proof of communication

    The date you spoke, the method, and a witness if there was one. A recording is required for Phone Call and Voice Mail.

    The monitoring period is calculated from this date.

  6. Attest and submit

    Read the attestation. Termination notices carry an additional confirmation about protected activity.

Before you submit

Run Ensuring a complete and accurate record against your draft — it’s the same criteria the People Team applies, so anything it flags is what would otherwise come back to you.

By Department

Every discussion in a department at once, rather than one team member at a time. Useful for spotting patterns, or checking what’s outstanding before a cycle closes.

  1. Click Load departments

    The dropdown stays disabled until you do. The list is built against your access, so it takes a few seconds — and only needs loading once per visit.

  2. Pick a department and a timeframe

    One department at a time. The timeframe limits how far back the search goes — it opens on the last week, and a shorter window returns faster.

  3. Narrow by status, if you want to

    Leave Status empty to include everything. Draft covers records where no status has been set yet.

    The Status filter narrows what loads before the tabs group it, so filtering to Finalized empties the In Progress tab. A note appears while a filter is active, as a reminder that an empty tab may be the filter rather than the data.

  4. Click Load discussions

    Results fill the same four tabs you see in By Team Member.

    Clicking Edit/Review collapses the discussions list to give the record room. Expand it again from the same header whenever you want it back.

    If the message says more records exist, narrow the timeframe. A department with a long history can hold more than one load can return.

FAQ

The handful of things that come up most.

The team member panel is empty or still loading

Give it a few seconds — it pulls from several places. If it’s still blank after that, click Start Over and select the person again.

The action buttons stay disabled until the panel has filled in. That’s deliberate: it stops you acting on information that isn’t there yet.

Validation is taking a long time

If a save runs past about fifteen seconds, try it again. A long session can slow the page down; reloading the tab usually clears it.

If it keeps happening, send the details to peopleops@capsule.com.

A section won’t validate and I can’t see why

The message under the section names the specific problem. The usual causes are text under 100 characters, more paragraphs than the section allows, or a single paragraph over the character limit.

If you pasted the text in, retype a few words and try again.

How do I add extra attachments to the record?

Create the record and save a few sections first — attachments go onto a record that already exists.

Find it under By Team Member > Review Existing Discussions > In Progress, or under By Department > In Progress.

Click View to open the record in a new tab. The Comments section there is where you add anything extra: photos, screenshots, emails, or any other supporting file.

I picked the wrong type or reason

Before you click Begin New Discussion, use Reset Type, Reason, and/or Level.

Afterwards those choices are written to the record and locked — cancel the discussion and start a new one instead. Copy your text across before you cancel.

A record came back to me

Its status is Sent Back and Waiting On now shows you. Open it with View to read the comments, then use Edit/Review to fix the section they named.

Everything you saved is still on the record. Re-save the section you changed, re-check the attestation, and submit again.

If the comments point at the warning level rather than the content, the record has to be canceled and recreated — level locks at creation.

Why is the review taking so long?

Reviews are normally decided within one to two business days.

If yours is taking longer than that, follow up with the People Team by commenting on the record and tagging us, or email peopleops@capsule.com.

I approved a termination notice but nothing was sent

That’s expected. A termination notice is the one type where the document waits for you: have the conversation first, then find the record on the Active tab and click Send Document, then Confirm.

Step 4 covers the full sequence, including scheduling the offboarding afterwards.

I need to record a discussion about myself

You can’t, and the dashboard will stop you. Contact peopleops@capsule.com.

I want to edit one discussion while drafting another

You can. Each section has its own switch, so turning both on puts Create New Discussion and Review Existing Discussions in view at once.

They keep separate state — work in one won’t disturb the other.

Someone who reports to me isn’t in my search results

The list of who you can reach is rebuilt several times a day rather than live, so a new hire or a recent reporting change can take a few hours to appear.

If they’re still missing after that, email peopleops@capsule.com — it usually means their reporting line needs correcting on their profile.

A tab in By Department is empty but shouldn’t be

Check whether you have a Status filter set. It narrows what loads before the tabs group it, so filtering to one status can empty the others entirely.

Clear the Status filter and load again to see everything.

What happens if the app is down?

Email peopleops@capsule.com with everything you would normally enter in the app — the type, the reason, the warning level if it’s a warning, the dates, and the details. What each type needs is in Which record do I create?

Write the formal notice yourself. Normally Rippling generates it from what you enter; with the app down, that has to be a document you produce.

What the document needs

  • Header. The team member’s name, title, department, and manager. The date of the notice. The discussion type, and the warning level if it’s a warning.
  • What happened. The date of the incident and the specifics — what was observed, who else it affected, and what the team member said when you spoke to them.
  • Prior matters. Any earlier discussions you’re relying on, with their dates and levels.
  • What happens next. The expectation going forward, the period it has to hold over, and the support you’re providing. Not needed on a termination notice.
  • Monitoring period. The end date, counted from the date of the conversation rather than the date you write this.
  • Closing language. Ask the People Team for the standard text for that type and level. It’s the same wording Rippling adds, and it should appear word for word.
  • Acknowledgement. A line for the team member’s signature and date, and one for yours, noting that signing acknowledges receipt rather than agreement.

Send both to the People Team, ask for approval if the discussion needs it, and copy us on whatever you send the team member. Rippling usually records that the document was delivered, so the CC is how we keep that trail.

Once the app is back up, the People Team can upload it into Rippling on your behalf.

Best practices: timing

Say something within two business days, and the same shift when you can. Feedback loses most of its value once the person has moved on, and a correction delivered three weeks late creates opportunity for more occurrences before the team member is aware.

Write the record within five business days. Anyone reading it later can see the gap between when it happened and when you documented it, and will likely have questions. The monitoring period also starts from the conversation date, so a late write-up shortens the period rather than delaying it.

Moving fast and moving right aren’t in conflict. If you need a day to check the route data or listen to the call, you can start the record and come back to it to submit for approval, or cancel it if needed.

If you do nothing, you’re likely to forget the specifics, and the recordings or meeting notes will be harder to find. In three weeks you’ll remember the general idea of what happened, but without the details it will be hard to defend. If this turns into a pattern later, you’ll need the early instances to show the pattern, and they won’t exist.

Best practices: picking the right type and reason

Unless a specific team’s policy says otherwise:

  • A single instance is a Memo of Conversation. A pattern is a Warning. One missed step, handled in a conversation, belongs in a memo. The same thing happening again after you’ve raised it belongs in a warning.
  • Don’t skip a level. A second or final warning shouldn’t be issued without a first or second already on file, and a termination notice shouldn’t be issued without a final warning before it.
Hint: check the Data Dashboard

Use the Data Dashboard to make an informed decision about potential patterns, or the level of warning that’s appropriate for repeated instances.
