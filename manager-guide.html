Running and recording a performance discussion

Discussions about attendance, performance, and behavior are how team members learn where they stand. Done well, they open a path forward. Done carelessly, they close one.

Plan a discussion

What you pick at the start decides which sections you fill in, how long the discussion stays in effect, and when the document reaches the team member.

Choose a type and reason to see what this discussion will involve.

Sections you'll fill in

    Type, reason, and level are locked once the record exists. Before you click Begin New Discussion, use Reset Type, Reason, and/or Level to change them. Afterwards, cancel the discussion and start a new one.

    Before you start

    Four things worth holding in mind, whatever the discussion is about.

    Assume they can improve

    A performance discussion is not meant to leave someone feeling scared, inadequate, or devalued. Go in assuming improvement is possible — the goal is to help them get there.

    Let them speak first

    Give the team member room to reflect on their own performance before you deliver yours. How they describe the situation tells you a great deal about their self-awareness and what they understand the job to require.

    Bring specifics, not impressions

    Dates, times, what was observed, who else was involved or affected. Specifics keep the conversation on deliverables and results. Generalizations invite argument about whether the thing even happened.

    Say what support looks like

    Name the coaching, tools, or resources you'll provide. A discussion that only describes a gap is a verdict; one that describes a path is an opportunity.

    Where things live

    The Performance Dashboard has two tabs you'll use.

    Data Dashboard

    Current attendance point balances and discussion history for your team, in two tables. The filters across the top narrow both at once — by employee, manager, department, work location, discussion type, warning level, status, or date range.

    Use this to see the shape of things before you act: whether a pattern exists, whether someone already has an active warning, how recent the last discussion was.

    Performance Discussion Actions

    Where you create and manage discussions. The left sidebar has two views: By Team Member, where you create and edit, and By Department, a read-only list across a whole department. By Team Member opens by default.

    Getting started

    1. Select the team member

      Use the search field at the top of By Team Member. You won't appear in your own results.

    2. Confirm their information

      Title, department, manager, work location, start date, and discussion counts appear in the right-hand panel. If anything's wrong, fix it on their profile first. The panel takes a moment to populate — wait for it before choosing an action.

    3. Choose an action

      Create a new discussion, or review existing ones. Opening one section hides the other. Each has a switch in case you need to hide it from view.

    Create a discussion

    The form is split into sections. Each one saves on its own.

    1. Fill in the required fields for each section

      Only the sections relevant to your type and reason appear. The planner above tells you which to expect.

    2. Click Validate & Save on each section

      When it succeeds, the button is replaced by Edit and the fields lock. That's your confirmation the section is saved.

    3. Complete the attestation and submit

      Every section must be saved before you can submit. Save records the attestation without starting approval; Submit sends it to the People team.

    After a successful submit, a confirmation appears and the Create New Discussion section clears itself, ready for the next one. The discussion you just submitted is in Review Existing Discussions.

    Dates and proof

    Dates of feedback and incident must fall on or before the discussion's creation date — and on or before the communication date, if one is set. The three no-call-no-show dates must be in ascending order with at least a day between each.

    A recording is required when the communication method is Phone Call or Voice Mail. Those are the only two with no written trail.

    Two things that will trip you up

    Pasting long text can fail validation. If a section won't save after a paste, retype a few words anywhere in the text and try again. That's usually enough.

    Editing a saved section unlocks it again. Click Edit, make your change, and save that section a second time. Nothing is lost — it just needs re-confirming.

    By department

    A read-only view of every discussion in a department, rather than one team member at a time.

    Useful for spotting patterns, or checking what's outstanding before a cycle closes.

    1. Click Load departments

      The dropdown stays disabled until you do. The list takes a few seconds to build and only needs loading once per visit.

    2. Pick a department and a timeframe

      One department at a time. The timeframe limits how far back the search goes — a shorter window returns faster.

    3. Narrow by status, if you want to

      Leave Status empty to include everything. Draft covers records where no status has been set yet.

    4. Click Load discussions

      Results are listed newest first, each with the team member's name. View opens the record in a new tab.

    This view is read-only — there's no Edit here. To change a discussion, find it under By Team Member.

    If the message says more records exist, narrow the timeframe. A department with a long history can hold more than one load can return.

    The department list is company-wide, not limited to your own team. Treat what you see accordingly.

    Termination notices

    The one type where the document waits for you.

    Every other discussion type sends its document automatically on approval. Termination notices don't — because the team member needs to hear this from you, not from an automated email.

    1. You prepare and submit

      Same as any other type.

    2. The People team reviews and approves

      You'll be notified when that happens.

    3. You have the conversation

      In person, directly. This step is yours and the system can't do it for you.

    4. Then you create the document

      Under Review Existing Discussions, on the Active tab: Send Document, then Confirm.

    Approval means the People team has signed off. It does not mean the team member has been told. Creating the document before that conversation notifies them before you've had the chance to.
